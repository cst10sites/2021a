The Mercator Projection

The Creator:

The Mercator Projection was created by Gerardus Mercator, a famous cartographer, in the year 1569. Gerardus was born in 1512, in the Belguim city of Ruplemonde, in the Flanders County into a poor family. His father was a shoemaker who he later worked for, and it is documented that gerardus started getting intereted by maps, when he began looking at one on he wall to pass the time whilst making shoes. After working for his father, he went to the Unversiy of Louvain where he graduated in 1532, with a degree in astronomy, mathematics, and geaography. He married his wife Barbara Schellekens in 1534, and had four children Rumold Mercator (Son)· Emerentia Mercator (Daughter)· Catharina Mercator (Daughter)· Dorothes Mercator (Daughter). His son went on to pursue cartography for little time, then went on to be a geographer in Southern Finland; His Daugter Emerentia, was also involved in cartograpy, as she often updated the map, when new discoeries occured, until her death in 1612. Gerardus was also famous for his detailled map of Europe, which was used for almost a century after he had died, it was dedicated to his friend Antoine Perrenot, who was a Cardinal.

The Projection:

The Mercator Projecetion has many benefits, as well as flaws, to its design. It is formattted as if you put a cylindar around the earth,and then you project all of the points onto the cylander, horizontally. Doing this will make it perfect on the equator, but it gradually gets more distorted as it gets closer to the poles. A common example of this is how Greenland looks like it is larger than Africa, when in actual fact Africa is 14 times bigger than it. Another benefitial aspect of it is the fact that it preserves straight lines, and parelles, which makes it one of the most used maps ever. With the straight lines, it is used in many satellite maps, including Google and Apple Maps, as it is easy to see the different roads as they are parallel to each other; another reason is the act that when zooming in, distortions do not occur, as this happens on many maps, whilst being expanded. I do believe that it is a reasonably good map for the reasons of the preserved lines, and the equator.
