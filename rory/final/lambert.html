The Lambert Conformal Conic

The Creator:

The Lambert Conformal Conic was created by Johann Heinrich Lambert, who was not actually a carotgraphy, but rather an mathematician. Johann was born in Mulhouse, France, now formally refered to as Alsace, on August 28, 1728, into a working poor family with a Swiss mother and a German father. Growing up, he left school at age 12, instead pusueing many different jobs across many different fields, but always found time to study, generally whilst working his jobs. Some of these professions, include, An assistant tailor to his father, a clerk at an iron works, a privat tutor, secretary to the editor of a famous newspaper. Eventually Johann used his earnings to tavel Europe, in hopes of trying to find a passion, or to at least learn some life lessons; luckily, he found what he was looking for.


Education:

After travelling europe for 2 years, Johann had gained many connections with well established mathematicians, streching form the German, to the Norwegian, and to the Italian States. With these connections, he made good use, and was inspired, so he wrote a book on Cosmology and Optics, two new topics, where he documented his learnings over the course of a whole year. This book caught the attention of the Prussian Academy of Sciences in Berlin, where he recieved a full scholarship, and made more important friendships, most famously with Euler, a famous Swiss scientist and mathematician. He Graduated the school in 1767 with degrees in mathematics, astronomy, physics, and philosophy at the age of 39, although over looked, in this time when he had reciently graduated, he spent most of his time focusing his time on astronomy and mathematics. In this time, he provided the first undisputed proof that Pi irrational, and although he dedicated a large portion of time to astronomy, he never made any major breakthoughs. After this one of the professers at the academy got him into learning about cartography, where he would dedicate the rest of his life.


Johann learned cartography quick, as he was an astronomer, and could often get a different of the world than ever seen before, like the Lambert Conformal Conic. He was fastinated by the idea of a conic map, a map that would be formed in the shape of a cone, with the centre point beong the north or south pole of the earth. In addition to that, it also created a bridge between astronomy and cartography, two fields that at this point he was skilled in, which kept him puruing the map even more. Finally in late 1771, only 1 year after learning the basics of cartography, he finished creating the Lambert Conformal Conic; but there was a problem, no publishing company would allow him to publish it, as they though it was to obsurd. Johann eventually went to apply for a job at the newspaper editor that he worked for, after all had rejected the idea of the projection, but the newspaper liked it, so they published it in autumn of 1772, and agaist belief, it was recieved well. After that, he continued to work in the field of cartography, up until his death in 1777, from a heart attack in Berlin. It was rumored that he was secretly, like most of his work, creating another improved conic projection at the time of his death, that would have fixed some of the errors.

The Projection:

Although the Lambert Conformal Conic has many errors, it also was some benefits. The main Problem with this map, is how unlike many other conic maps to followed it does not have equal area between the continents, as Africa appears much larger than the whole of North and Sout America. This mistake is forgivable, a this was the original conic shaped map, and it was only recently when a team of scientists and cartographers were able to develop one with perfect area. That being said, all of the geometric preportions laterally are perfect, but the longitude is a different and much worse story, as it gets larger toward the base of the cone, the south pole. The map has curving parallels which provide a similar look to a globe which can help viewers visualize the map better, if they have seen a globe for comparison. Lambert Conformal Conic also boasts two standard parallels at 33°N and 45°N, which help to minimalize the downward stretch as much as possible. With all of this being said, i do believe that it is a shame that this map does not get utilized more often.
