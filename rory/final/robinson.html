The Robinson Projection

The Creator:

The Robinson Projection was created by a Canadian born cartogapher and geographer, Arthur Robinson. Arthur was born in Montreal Canada, on January 5th, 1915, into an American family, but moved to London when he was three-years old in 1918. He moved back Oxford Ohio, in the USA where he recieved his post-secondary education at Miami University, graduating in 1936 with a bachelors degree. Whilst doing his undergraduate work, he started to become interested in cartography, and practiced drawing miniature maps in faculty text book that he had stolen. During this period of time, he was busy attending the University of Wisconsin to get a masters degree in geography in 1938.


Wartime:

During WWII Arthur robinson served as a part of the American Military, where he served as the map division director for the Office of Strategic Servies, normally refered to a the OSS. When he began working with the OSS, there were only geographers, no cartographers, under these cirrcumstances, he managed to intersted the geographers with his mapping, so the group created techniques never seen before. He single handedly saw the development of abut 5000 hand drawn maps; in addition to going to the Allied conference held in Quebec and Cairo. Although he never fought in the war, he was still an integral part of he allied forces, and helped plan Operation Overlord (D-day), and the invasion of Italy, which majorly swayed the war In 1946, Arthur Robinson recieved the Legion of Merit from the United States Military, the highest honour that could be given for his role.


After The War:

After the War, Robinson went on to earn a Ph.D from Ohio University in cartography and geography in 1949, after which, he joined the geography department at University of Wisconsin. There he spent the rest of his career in acedemia and research, where the program has conferred over 110 masters degrees, and 30 doctoral degrees, and later they named the library in his honour. In 1952 Arthur published a book called The Look Of Maps, which consists of his doctoral researh, where he urged all cartographers to consider the function of the map as a part of the process of creation. Finally, in 1963, after Randy McNally, a good friend from the military, asked him to create a map with limited distortion, which he proceded to do. Arthur worked all teh way up until 1999, when he retired, then five years later on october 10, 2004, he passed away from natural causes at the age of 89.

The Projection:

Although the map looks different, it is atually one of the most well rounded maps as it is good in many catagoties. The main thing about it, is how it is niether a conformal map, or an equal area map, as he felt that this produced a better overall viewing of it. On the map, the medians gently curl, hence, stretching the poles into long lines, rather than point that are used on most maps currently With this in mind, it is clear to see that there is high distortion close to the poles, but it quickley gets better moving away form them, downwards. The straight parallels create immense angular at high latitudes, near the outer edges of it, which is immposible to avoid on all round maps.
